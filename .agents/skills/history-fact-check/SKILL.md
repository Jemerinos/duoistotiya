# Independent history fact-check

## Independence
Review the proposed lesson as an editor, not as its co-author. Do not assume that polished prose or another agent's confidence means a claim is correct. Do not rewrite the whole lesson before identifying evidence-backed issues.

## Procedure
1. Read the complete lesson and identify checkable claims: dates, people, places, numbers, quotations, laws, battles, institutions, causal explanations, and claims about what historians agree on.
2. Verify the most consequential claims against accessible sources. Use primary sources when they can establish the relevant evidence, and reputable scholarly, archive, museum, university, or educational sources for context. Seek corroboration for disputed or high-impact claims.
3. For each issue, record the exact claim or a short locator, severity, what is wrong or unsupported, source(s) checked, and a proposed correction. Do not fabricate citations or claim to have opened a source that was inaccessible.
4. Check chronology, geographical context, transliteration/names, units, and whether the described institution or term existed in that period.
5. Test causal logic: distinguish background conditions, triggers, actors' decisions, mechanisms, and outcomes. Flag monocausal explanations, teleology, unsupported motives, and claims that confuse sequence with causation.
6. Separate primary evidence, later reconstructions, and scholarly interpretations. Identify anachronisms, false balance, nationalist framing, and unsupported certainty without imposing anachronistic moral simplicity.
7. Check whether important uncertainty or scholarly disagreement is hidden. Where sources disagree, report the disagreement and avoid false precision.
8. Assess clarity for a school-age reader and whether the text actually explains terms and consequences rather than merely naming them.
9. Return a structured verdict: PASS, PASS WITH NOTES, or NEEDS REVISION. A PASS means no material issue was found in the checks performed; it does not mean every sentence has been exhaustively verified.
10. After corrections, re-check only the changed claims plus any affected downstream statements, dates, quizzes, and answer keys.

## Severity
- BLOCKER: major factual error, fabricated source/quote, misleading core chronology, or unsupported claim central to the lesson.
- MAJOR: meaningful error or omission that distorts causality, actors, or consequences.
- MINOR: wording, nuance, precision, or context improvement that does not reverse the lesson's main account.

## Output format
- Verdict and scope of review.
- Blockers, majors, and minors as separate lists.
- Claim / concern / source checked / recommended correction.
- Claims not verified and why.
- Quiz, glossary, image, or timeline inconsistencies.
- Residual uncertainty. Never conceal a blocker to make the lesson look complete.
